Human Anatomy & Physiology - Quick Review

8 Short-Answer Questions covering Chapters 1, 2, and 3.

1. How does anatomy differ from physiology, and what is the relationship between structure and function?

Answer:

Anatomy is the study of the structure or physical form of the body, whereas physiology is the study of how body parts function. The relationship is that structure determines function—a body part's function depends directly on how it is built.

2. Explain the difference between catabolism and anabolism with a simple physiological example.

Answer:

Catabolism breaks down complex chemical compounds into simpler ones (releasing energy), while anabolism builds complex molecules from smaller components (requiring energy). Example: Digestion catabolizes proteins in food into amino acids, which are then used by cells to anabolize new muscle tissue proteins.

3. Why are the heads of phospholipid molecules in the cell membrane oriented outward while the tails face inward?

Answer:

The phospholipid heads are hydrophilic ("water-loving") and interact with the watery intracellular and extracellular fluids. The tails are hydrophobic ("water-fearing") and avoid water by pointing inward toward the center of the membrane bilayer.

4. How does the active transport of the Sodium-Potassium pump (Na+ / K+ ATPase) differ from passive facilitated diffusion?

Answer:

Facilitated diffusion moves substances passively down their concentration gradient without energy. The Sodium-Potassium pump uses ATP energy for active transport to move Sodium out of the cell and Potassium into the cell against their concentration gradients.

5. Contrast transcription and translation in terms of what is produced and where in the cell each stage occurs.

Answer:

• Transcription: Occurs in the nucleus; copies genetic code from DNA into mRNA.
• Translation: Occurs in the cytoplasm (at ribosomes); decodes mRNA into a sequence of amino acids to construct a protein.

6. Compare the chromosome counts and genetic outcomes of daughter cells produced by mitosis versus meiosis.

Answer:

• Mitosis: Produces 2 genetically identical diploid cells (46 chromosomes each) for body growth/repair.
• Meiosis: Produces 4 genetically different haploid cells (23 chromosomes each) for gametes (sperm/egg).

7. How does the arrangement of collagen fibers differ between dense regular and dense irregular connective tissue?

Answer:

In dense regular connective tissue, collagen fibers are tightly packed in parallel bundles (providing strength in one direction, like in tendons). In dense irregular connective tissue, collagen fibers are arranged randomly in multiple directions (providing tensile strength in many directions, like in the dermis).

8. What are the two main cell types in nervous tissue, and what are their basic roles?

Answer:

• Neurons: Nerve cells that generate and transmit electrical nerve impulses.
• Neuroglia: Non-conductive cells that support, nourish, insulate, and protect neurons.
